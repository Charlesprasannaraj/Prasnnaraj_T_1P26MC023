Solar System / Small bodies

Ancient clues in orbit

Asteroids, comets and dwarf planets

Smaller objects preserve clues about how the Solar System formed. They range from rocky asteroids to icy comets and dwarf planets such as Pluto.

AsteroidsMostly rocky or metallic
CometsIcy bodies that can grow glowing comas and tails
Dwarf planetsRound worlds that orbit the Sun

Asteroids

Asteroids are rocky or metallic objects that orbit the Sun. Many are found in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, though others travel on different paths through the Solar System.

Comets

Comets contain ice, dust and rocky material. When a comet approaches the Sun, warming releases gas and dust that can form a bright coma and tail. The tail points generally away from the Sun.

Dwarf planets

Dwarf planets orbit the Sun and are massive enough for gravity to make them nearly round, but they have not cleared their orbital neighbourhood of other objects. Pluto is one example.
